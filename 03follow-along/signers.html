Who Were the Signers?

Famous Signers

Some of the most famous and historically important signers of the Declaration of Independence were:

Thomas Jefferson
Virginia; wrote the main draft of the Declaration of Independence. Later became the 3rd U.S. president.
John Hancock
Massachusetts; president of the Continental Congress. Famous for his large, distinctive signature. Later became governor of Massachusetts.
Benjamin Franklin
Pennsylvania; famous scientist, inventor, diplomat, and statesman. He was 70 years old when he signed.
John Adams
Massachusetts; major advocate for independence. Later became the 2nd U.S. president.
Samuel Adams
Massachusetts; influential revolutionary and political organizer. He was also John Adams's cousin.
John Witherspoon
New Jersey; a minister and president of the College of New Jersey (now Princeton). He was the only college president to sign the Declaration.
Charles Carroll of Carrollton
Maryland; one of the wealthiest men in the colonies and the last surviving signer of the Declaration.
Button Gwinnett
Georgia; a revolutionary leader who later served as governor of Georgia.
George Walton
Georgia; one of the youngest signers and later served as governor of Georgia.
Edward Rutledge
South Carolina; signed the Declaration at only 26 years old, making him the youngest signer.

One interesting detail: 56 men signed the Declaration, representing the 13 colonies. Some were extremely famous afterward, but many were relatively obscure today.
